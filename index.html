What must be tested before changing the allocation rule?
  • Recover original release vintages, with each value available before its decision time.
  • Compare net total-return portfolios with NIFTY buy-and-hold, a fixed 60/40 portfolio and the valuation-only rule.
  • Report drawdown, CAGR, expected shortfall, time under water and turnover together.
  • Test lump-sum deployment, monthly contributions and inflation-linked retirement withdrawals separately.
  • Compare short-duration debt with long-duration bonds, including instrument costs and applicable taxes.
  • Evaluate macro challengers and all attempted variants without automatically promoting a backtest winner.

These evaluations are planned, not completed. First process review: 12 September 2027. A review date or 12 overlapping annual outcomes does not establish statistical validation.

Each decision uses the most recent NIFTY observation strictly before the calendar 1st. Bond yields use an assumed two-month lag: archived RBI tenor yields before OECD coverage, then OECD monthly yields. Yield observations are 29–32 days old at these decision dates. This lag is an assumption, not a verified publication delay or a claim of conservative returns. OECD observations are monthly series, not daily closing yields. Original publication vintages are unverified. Legacy V3.6 reference parameters are applied retrospectively; this table does not use or validate the live V3.10 calibration.

V3.6 adds an official India domestic regime block using MoSPI/PIB CPI inflation, MoSPI/PIB industrial-production growth, and the RBI policy repo rate. CPI is scored against the RBI 4% inflation target, IIP against a transparent 5% growth anchor, and real policy stance against a 1% real-rate anchor. These are deliberately called economic anchors rather than z-scores because the CPI and IIP base revisions shorten directly comparable history.
